Single-Phase Transformer

A transformer is a static electrical device that transfers AC electrical energy from one circuit to another through mutual electromagnetic induction without changing frequency.

Transformer Parameters

Primary Voltage 230 V
Secondary Voltage 115 V
Primary Turns 1000
Secondary Turns 500
Frequency 50 Hz
Mode Step-Down

Working Principle

When an AC voltage is applied to the primary winding, an alternating current produces an alternating magnetic flux in the iron core.

This alternating flux links the secondary winding and induces an EMF according to Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction.

e = −N dΦ/dt

Thus, electrical energy is transferred through magnetic coupling.

Transformation Ratio

V₂ / V₁ = N₂ / N₁
I₂ / I₁ = N₁ / N₂

For an ideal transformer:

V₁I₁ = V₂I₂

EMF Equation

E₁ = 4.44 f N₁ Φm
E₂ = 4.44 f N₂ Φm

where:
f = frequency in Hz
N = number of turns
Φm = maximum flux in Wb

Transformer Losses

Loss Cause Dependence
Copper loss Winding resistance I²R
Hysteresis loss Repeated magnetization Frequency & flux
Eddy-current loss Circulating currents in core Flux & frequency
PCu = I₁²R₁ + I₂²R₂
Pcore = Physteresis + Peddy

Transformer Efficiency

η = Output Power / Input Power × 100
η = V₂I₂cosφ / (V₂I₂cosφ + Losses) × 100

Maximum efficiency occurs approximately when:

Copper Loss = Core Loss

Voltage Regulation

Voltage regulation indicates the change in secondary terminal voltage when the transformer changes from no-load to full-load condition.

% Regulation = (E₂ − V₂) / V₂ × 100

E₂ = secondary no-load voltage
V₂ = secondary full-load terminal voltage

Voltage Regulation Simulator



Regulation = 4.35 %

Maximum Efficiency

For a transformer, maximum efficiency occurs when:

PCu = PCore

If the core loss is 40 W, maximum efficiency occurs at the load condition where copper loss is also 40 W.
