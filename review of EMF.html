1. Basic Magnetic Principles

A magnetic field is a region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor in which magnetic force can be experienced. The magnetic field is represented using magnetic flux lines. Magnetic flux lines:

• Travel from North to South outside a magnet.
• Form closed paths.
• Never intersect each other.
• Are more closely spaced where the magnetic field is stronger.

2. Important Magnetic Quantities

Quantity Symbol Unit Meaning
Magnetic Flux Φ Weber (Wb) Total magnetic field passing through an area
Flux Density B Tesla (T) Flux per unit area
Magnetic Field Strength H A/m Magnetizing force per unit length
MMF F A-turns Force producing magnetic flux
Reluctance ℜ A/Wb Opposition to magnetic flux
Permeability μ H/m Ability of material to conduct magnetic flux

3. Magnetic Flux Density

B = Φ / A
where:
B = magnetic flux density in tesla
Φ = magnetic flux in weber
A = cross-sectional area in m²
A higher magnetic flux passing through a smaller area produces higher magnetic flux density.

4. Relationship Between B and H

B = μH
For a magnetic material:
μ = μ₀ μᵣ
where:
μ₀ = permeability of free space
μᵣ = relative permeability

5. Magnetic Circuit Concept

The magnetic circuit is analogous to an electrical circuit.
Electrical: I = V / R
Magnetic: Φ = MMF / Reluctance
Therefore:
Φ = NI / ℜ

6. Reluctance

ℜ = l / (μA)
where:
l = magnetic path length
μ = permeability
A = cross-sectional area

Therefore:
Φ = NI / ℜ

8. Electromagnetic Induction

Electromagnetic induction is the phenomenon in which an EMF is produced in a conductor or coil whenever the magnetic flux linking the conductor changes.

The change in flux may be produced by:
• Relative motion between conductor and magnetic field
• Changing current in a nearby coil
• Rotation of a conductor in a magnetic field
• Movement of a magnet relative to a coil

9. Types of Induced EMF

Dynamically Induced EMF

Produced when a conductor moves through a magnetic field and cuts magnetic flux.

Example: DC generator

Statically Induced EMF

Produced when the conductor is stationary but the magnetic flux linking it changes.

Example: Transformer

11. Faraday's Laws of Electromagnetic Induction

First Law

Whenever the magnetic flux linking a circuit changes, an EMF is induced in the circuit. If the circuit is closed, an induced current flows.

Second Law

The magnitude of induced EMF is proportional to the rate of change of flux linkage.
e = -N dΦ/dt
For average values:
e = -N (ΔΦ / Δt)

12. Lenz's Law

Lenz's law determines the direction of induced EMF and current. The induced current always produces a magnetic effect that opposes the change in magnetic flux responsible for producing it.
Easy way to remember:

The induced effect always opposes the change that produced it.

13. Fleming's Right-Hand Rule

Fleming's right-hand rule is used to determine the direction of induced current in a generator.

Stretch the thumb, first finger and second finger of the right hand mutually perpendicular:

Thumb → Motion of conductor
First finger → Magnetic field
Second finger → Induced current

14. Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

Fleming's left-hand rule is used to determine the direction of force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.

First finger → Magnetic field
Second finger → Current
Thumb → Force / Motion

It is particularly useful for understanding the operating principle of a DC motor.

17. Numerical Example – Magnetic Flux

Problem 1

A magnetic circuit has an MMF of 500 A-turns and a reluctance of 2.5 × 10⁵ A/Wb. Calculate the magnetic flux.
Given:
MMF = 500 A-turns
ℜ = 2.5 × 10⁵ A/Wb
Formula:
Φ = MMF / ℜ
Substitution:
Φ = 500 / (2.5 × 10⁵)
Φ = 0.002 Wb = 2 mWb

18. Numerical Example – Flux Density

Problem 2

A magnetic core carries a flux of 0.015 Wb through a cross-sectional area of 0.005 m². Determine the flux density.
Given:
Φ = 0.015 Wb
A = 0.005 m²
Formula:
B = Φ / A
Substitution:
B = 0.015 / 0.005
B = 3 T

19. Numerical Example – MMF

Problem 3

A magnetic coil has 500 turns and carries a current of 2 A. Determine the MMF.
Given: N = 500 turns
I = 2 A
Formula:
MMF = NI
Calculation:
MMF = 500 × 2
MMF = 1000 A-turns

20. Numerical Example – Faraday's Law

Problem 4

A coil of 200 turns experiences a change in magnetic flux from 0.02 Wb to 0.005 Wb in 0.05 s. Determine the magnitude of the average induced EMF.
Given:
N = 200
Φ₁ = 0.02 Wb
Φ₂ = 0.005 Wb
Δt = 0.05 s
Flux change:
ΔΦ = Φ₂ − Φ₁
ΔΦ = 0.005 − 0.02
ΔΦ = −0.015 Wb
Magnitude of EMF:
e = N |ΔΦ| / Δt
e = 200 × 0.015 / 0.05
e = 60 V

21. Numerical Example – Sinusoidal Flux

Problem 5

A coil of 500 turns is linked by a sinusoidal flux having a maximum value of 0.01 Wb at a frequency of 50 Hz. Determine the RMS induced EMF.
Given:
N = 500 turns
Φm = 0.01 Wb
f = 50 Hz
Formula:
E = 4.44 f N Φm
Substitution:
E = 4.44 × 50 × 500 × 0.01
E = 1110 V

22. Numerical Example – Magnetic Field Strength

Problem 6

A magnetic coil has 800 turns and carries 1.5 A current. The mean magnetic path length is 0.4 m. Determine H.
Formula:
H = NI / l
Calculation:
H = (800 × 1.5) / 0.4
H = 3000 A/m

23. Numerical Example – Reluctance

Problem 7

A magnetic core has a mean length of 0.5 m, cross-sectional area of 0.002 m² and permeability of 0.001 H/m. Calculate its reluctance.
Formula:
ℜ = l / (μA)
Substitution:
ℜ = 0.5 / (0.001 × 0.002)
ℜ = 250,000 A/Wb
