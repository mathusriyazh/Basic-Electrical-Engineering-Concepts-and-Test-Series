1. Inverter System – Block Diagram

Inverter: An inverter is a power-electronic converter that converts DC electrical power into AC electrical power.

Typical sequence: DC Source → DC Link → Switching Bridge → LC Filter → AC Load

The semiconductor switches are controlled using PWM or SPWM. The switching operation creates an AC waveform from the DC input. The LC filter removes high-frequency switching components.

3. Inverter Switching Operation

Positive half cycle:
S1 and S4 are ON while S2 and S3 are OFF. The load voltage becomes approximately +Vdc.

Negative half cycle:
S2 and S3 are ON while S1 and S4 are OFF. The load voltage becomes approximately −Vdc.

By rapidly switching the devices using PWM/SPWM, the fundamental component becomes approximately sinusoidal.

4. Uninterruptible Power Supply – General Block Diagram

A UPS provides electrical power to a critical load even when the utility supply fails or becomes abnormal. In a typical online UPS:

Utility AC → Rectifier → DC Bus → Inverter → Critical AC Load

The battery is connected to the DC bus through the charging and battery-management system. When the utility supply fails:

Battery → DC Bus → Inverter → Critical AC Load

Therefore, the load can continue to receive AC power without waiting for a conventional generator to start.

10. Waveform Concept

Square-wave inverter: Output changes directly between positive and negative voltage levels.

PWM inverter: High-frequency switching pulses are generated to control the fundamental output voltage.

SPWM: A sinusoidal reference is compared with a high-frequency triangular carrier to generate gate pulses.

11. Important Inverter Formulas

DC Input Power: Pdc = Vdc × Idc
AC Output Power: Pac = Vrms × Irms × cosφ
Inverter Efficiency: η = (Pac / Pdc) × 100 %
Output RMS Voltage: Vrms = Vpeak / √2
DC-to-AC Conversion: DC → Switching Bridge → AC
Modulation Index: m = Vreference / Vcarrier

12. UPS Formulas

UPS Efficiency: ηUPS = (Pload / Pinput) × 100 %
Battery Energy: Ebattery = Vbattery × Ah
Backup Time: t = (Vbattery × Ah × η) / Pload
Required Battery Capacity: Ah = (Pload × t) / (Vbattery × η)
Apparent Power: S = Vrms × Irms
Power Factor: PF = P / S

14. Worked Example

Problem: A 48 V, 100 Ah battery supplies a 1000 W load through a UPS. The overall conversion efficiency is 90%. Determine the approximate backup time.

Step 1 – Battery Energy
E = V × Ah
E = 48 × 100 = 4800 Wh
Step 2 – Usable Energy
Eusable = 4800 × 0.90 = 4320 Wh
Step 3 – Backup Time
t = Eusable / Pload
t = 4320 / 1000 = 4.32 hours

15. Example: Inverter Efficiency

Suppose:
Vdc = 48 V
Idc = 25 A
Pac = 1100 W
DC input power:
Pdc = 48 × 25 = 1200 W
Therefore:
η = (1100 / 1200) × 100
η = 91.67 %
